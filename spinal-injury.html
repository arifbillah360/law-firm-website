How Do I Know I Have a Spinal Injury Case?

Not every spinal injury will lead to a successful compensation claim. Under California law, your attorney must be able to prove that your injuries were caused by an accident—and that another party is legally responsible for that accident.

Spinal injuries also tend to trigger aggressive insurance pushback. Insurers often argue that pain is "pre-existing," that imaging is "inconclusive," or that your future limitations are "speculative." If the injury involves paralysis or permanent impairment, it may overlap with catastrophic injury litigation—and the defense will fight even harder.

At Peerali Law, we look for the following elements when reviewing a spinal injury case:

  • Clear medical diagnosis supported by imaging (MRI/CT/X-ray) and specialist evaluation
  • Treatment timeline that connects the accident to the onset and progression of symptoms
  • Evidence proving how the accident happened (police reports, incident reports, scene photos)
  • Independent witnesses, CCTV, dashcam, or other objective proof of fault
  • Documentation of work restrictions, disability impact, and loss of earning capacity
  • Consistent pain and symptom reporting across providers (to prevent "credibility" attacks)
  • Strong indicators of third-party liability (unsafe property, negligent driving, defective conditions, etc.)

These cases require a deeper level of legal strategy than standard personal injury claims. The attorney must not only prove liability, but also quantify the entire future impact of the injury.
