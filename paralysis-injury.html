How Do I Know I Have a Paralysis Injury Case?

Not every paralysis injury will mean you can pursue damages through a personal injury lawsuit. Under California law, you have to prove that a third party was responsible for the accident which caused your injuries.

Furthermore, when you suffer catastrophic injuries severe enough change the course of a person's life—such as a paralysis injury—an insurance company will fight hard to dispute liability. This is because paralysis injury settlements are often higher, and therefore insurance companies want to mitigate any potential payments.

At Peerali Law, we look for the following elements when reviewing a paralysis injury case:

  • Emergency response records and incident reports documenting the initial injury event
  • Medical imaging and specialist evaluations confirming spinal cord or neurological damage
  • Surgical records, rehabilitation plans and long-term prognosis reports
  • Witness statements, photographs, or video footage that corroborate how the accident happened
  • Evidence of third-party fault (unsafe property conditions, negligent drivers, defective products, or employer negligence)
  • Expert testimony (medical specialists, accident reconstruction, safety engineers) supporting causation and liability
  • Documentation of permanent impairment, disability classification, and future care needs

Paralysis cases require a deeper level of legal strategy than standard personal injury claims. The attorney must not only prove liability, but also quantify the entire future impact of the injury.

Types of Paralysis Injuries

There are five primary types of paralysis, all of which can be either partial or complete.

Partial paralysis means a person has some control over the muscles in that part of the body. Complete paralysis is when there is no control over a body part whatsoever.

There are five distinct categories of paralysis, namely:

  • Monoplegia—loss of function in one limb
  • Diplegia—paralysis in the same area on both sides of the body
  • Hemiplegia—loss of function on one side of the body
  • Paraplegia—paralysis in both legs
  • Tetraplegia—paralysis in all four limbs

All types of paralysis injuries can be classed as catastrophic in California law, even if the symptoms are only temporary. This is why you'll require a boutique law firm specialized in complex and catastrophic injuries, like Peerali Law.
